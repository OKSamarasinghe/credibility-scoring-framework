      From a pasted posting to an explainable verdict

      Every score comes with the reasoning behind it — never a bare number.

      Submit the posting

      Paste the description text, and fill in any structured details you know — title, location, salary, employment type. Anything left blank stays genuinely unknown, not guessed.

      The model reads it

      A fine-tuned language model reads the text together with four categories of evidence — content cues, identity cues, metadata completeness, and text–metadata consistency — rather than judging the words in isolation.

      Get a calibrated, explained score

      The model's prediction is calibrated into a 0–100 credibility score, then explained with specific reasons, matched phrases, and a checklist of what to verify yourself.

      Four categories of evidence, fused together

      Not just what the posting says — how complete it is, and whether it agrees with itself.

      E1

      Text & content cues

      Scans the wording for scam-style language — artificial urgency, guaranteed pay, requests to pay money upfront.

      E2

      Identity & contact cues

      Looks at how the employer identifies itself — a company profile, a specific location, and how applicants are asked to get in touch.

      E3

      Metadata completeness

      Checks which of the standard posting details are actually filled in, since postings missing basic information carry more risk.

      E4

      Text–metadata consistency

      Flags contradictions between the free text and the structured fields — a posting calling itself remote while also listing an office location, for example.

      About this project

      JobCred is the applied artefact of a Final Year Research Project, An Explainable Credibility Scoring Framework for Online Recruitment Posts Using Multi-Signal Evidence and Consistency Verification. It combines a fine-tuned transformer model with structured evidence and consistency-verification rules, so every score comes with a stated, checkable reason rather than a bare number.
